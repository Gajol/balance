Pattern catalog / PAT-004

A message says that something happened, which item it is about, and where to go. It never carries the content itself.

  • Status: Proposed, pending EA confirmation
  • Version 0.1 · 2026-10-24
  • Applies: Accessible by default, Bilingual parity, Secure by design
  • Main layer: Content and governance

Supports: Reminders and digests reach people without carrying content out of the intranet.

  • Messages and reminders
  • Security and isolation
  • Ownership and freshness

Found by the Solution Architect in the solution architecture (where it governs notification email). Not named by the EA. Offered as a candidate pattern, pending EA confirmation.

The rule

DO

Send a pointer, not payload message: the event, the item's title, a link and a date. Let the person open the link, signed in, to see the content.

DON'T

Paste the content into the message so people need not click. The copy then outlives every permission change and sits in mailboxes, phones and archives we do not control.

When this comes up

  • The intranet must reach people: a review is due, a page was flagged, something is waiting. Email is the only reachable channel in the first release (Notifications through the internal mail relay, No mobile in the first release).
  • Email is the one place where information leaves the intranet's access model. Inside the site, CMS decides who sees a page. In an inbox, forwarding rules, phone sync and local archives decide.
  • Stakeholders will ask why the email does not just say what changed. It sounds helpful and it is the easy path.

What pulls in different directions:

  • Short, useful messages, against the temptation to include everything.
  • Staff convenience, against records and access-to-information exposure.

Picture it

The experience

A content owner gets a short message that tells them what needs doing and where. They decide in the intranet, signed in, where the choice is logged.

Design rules for this pattern

  • Say what happened, which item, and where to go. Nothing more.
  • Put English and French in one message.
  • Never accept an action by reply.
  • Keep subject lines to the event.

How to test the experience

Check these with real staff, in English and in French, and with a screen reader. The pattern is met if the answer to each is yes.

  • Does every test message contain only event, title (or the suppressed phrase), link, date and role names?
  • Does a reply to a test message change nothing in the CMS?
  • Is a title-suppressed page absent from the subject and the body?
  • Does a screen reader read the link as a destination, not "click here"?
  • Can the owner finish keep, refresh or retire in three steps or fewer?
How it works
  1. Name the event. List the few events that may send mail: review due, flagged, correction required, queued, archived. Anything else needs a record.
  2. Write the template as a pointer. Allowed: event, page title, link, due date, affected language, who acted (role and name), a count. Not allowed: body text, comments, attachments, personal information about a third party.
  3. Keep the subject line to the event. No content in the subject. It shows in preview panes on shared screens.
  4. Suppress sensitive titles. Content types and sections marked title suppression send "A page you own needs review" and a link, nothing more.
  5. Send both languages in one message. English block, then French block. Never guess the recipient's language.
  6. Prefer a digest. Daily for the review queue, weekly for coming review dates. Send at once only for a required correction or an unpublish.
  7. Act in the intranet only. No approve, publish, retire or clear-a-flag by email reply. The action happens signed in, where it is logged.
  8. Enforce it in code review. The no-content rule is tested on the template. A guide alone will not hold it.
Definition cards

Hover or focus any dotted term in this page to see the same definition.

Pointer, not payload

Send or store a link and the minimum needed to recognise the item, not the content itself.

Why it matters hereKeeps content inside the system that owns it and its access rules. Already the rule for notification email.

Digest

One message that bundles many items, such as everything waiting for review, instead of one message per item.

Why it matters hereA stream of single messages trains staff to filter them into a folder they never open.

Isolated network

A network with no connection to the internet. Nothing can be downloaded at install or run time.

Why it matters hereEvery tool, package, font and asset must already be inside. A pattern that needs an outside service is not allowed.

Title suppression

A marking on a content type or section meaning its page titles must not appear in notifications, because the title itself would disclose the matter.

Why it matters hereA title such as a workforce adjustment is the disclosure. Communications sets the marking with the tier.

Journey map

A picture of one person's path through a task, step by step, with what they do, think and feel at each step.

Why it matters hereIt shows where an experience breaks, so we fix the right step and can test it with real staff.

Wireframe

A plain sketch of a screen that shows what goes where. It leaves out final colours, wording and branding on purpose.

Why it matters herePeople can agree on layout, language switch and reading order before anything is built, and a vendor can show the same screen back.

Trade-offs

What we gain

  • Nothing sensitive can leak by forwarding, because there is nothing to forward.
  • Permissions are checked when the link is clicked, so a person who loses access loses the content.
  • Fewer copies of the same information to find under access-to-information requests.
  • Short messages get read.

What it costs

  • One more click for the reader.
  • Pressure to "just include the text" will return with every new template.
  • Title suppression needs a decision on each sensitive content type.
  • Template tests and review take developer time.

When not to use it

  • The message is itself a formal record, such as a legal notice. Use the formal channel, not this pattern.
  • An emergency or safety alert. Use the internal emergency process, outside the intranet.
Checks every pattern must pass

Bilingual (official languages law)

One message holds an English block and a French block, with the same facts in the same order. The send log records which language versions were affected. Parity is measured on the link target, which has both versions before it publishes.

Accessible (WCAG 2.1 AA)

Plain text or simple accessible HTML that meets WCAG 2.1 AA. Link text names the destination ("Open the page to review"), never "click here". Short messages are easier to read with a screen reader.

On-premises, no outside connection

Mail goes only through the internal mail relay. Nothing is sent to an outside address and no tracking pixels are used. Templates are part of the signed release.

FLAG On-premises conflict

The government notification service is a cloud service and is not available on the isolated network. The mail system is the channel (Notifications through the internal mail relay). Do not propose a hosted email or push service.

How to test a vendor proposal

Ask the vendor to show, not tell. The pattern is met if the answer to each is yes.

  • Can you show a notification template and the automated test that fails when body text is added?
  • Does every message carry an English block and a French block with the same facts?
  • Can a page be marked so its title never appears in a message?
  • Can you show that replying to a message cannot approve, publish or retire anything?
  • Is every send recorded in the CMS with event, recipient and time?
  • Can a daily digest replace a stream of single messages?

Proposed decision record

Pointer, not payload applies to every outbound message

Every message the intranet sends to another channel carries a pointer, never content. Notifications through the internal mail relay sets this for email. This record would extend it to digests, task links and any later channel. An exception needs its own record.

Status: Proposed, pending EA confirmation. Not yet logged.
