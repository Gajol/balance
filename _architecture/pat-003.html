Pattern catalog / PAT-003

When staff need something we cannot link to or copy, we publish a short page about it. The page has a title, an owner and a review date, so it is found, measured and kept fresh like everything else.

  • Status: Proposed, pending EA confirmation
  • Version 0.1 · 2026-10-24
  • Applies: Bilingual parity, Automated, Transparent
  • Main layer: Website experience

Supports: Staff find an owned, reviewed page for a system they cannot reach, instead of a dead link.

  • Search and findability
  • Integration with other systems
  • Ownership and freshness

Named by the EA on 2026-10-24 as the 'reference' pattern. This page assumes a reference card is a short owned page that stands for something we cannot crawl or link. The EA to confirm that is what was meant.

The rule

DO

Publish a reference card for any system, service or destination staff need but we cannot crawl or link to. It says what it is, who it is for, and how to reach it, in English and French.

DON'T

Leave a bare link to somewhere the network cannot reach, or leave staff to find out by searching and getting nothing. A dead link has no title in search, no owner and no review date.

When this comes up

  • Staff look for things such as pay, a central agency site or an internal system that is not reachable from the isolated network.
  • A link that does not open is a dead end. Search shows nothing, and nobody owns the gap.
  • Some destinations are reachable only by phone, by form or in person.

What pulls in different directions:

  • Helping staff find the thing, against not being able to link to it.
  • A page per destination, against the cost of keeping each one current.
  • One rule for every destination, against some being reachable and some not.

Picture it

The experience

When staff need a system they cannot open from this network, a dead link wastes their time and no one owns the fix. A reference card says what the system is, who can use it and how to reach it, and it has an owner.

Design rules for this pattern

  • Put reachability right after the title, in words.
  • Never show a link that will not open from this network.
  • Show the owner position and the review date on the card.
  • Use the same fields and order in English and French.

How to test the experience

Check these with real staff, in English and in French, and with a screen reader. The pattern is met if the answer to each is yes.

  • Can a tester say, within 10 seconds, whether they can use the system from their desk?
  • Does the card show no link when the system is not reachable?
  • Do English and French cards have the same fields, owner and review date?
  • Does the card appear in the review report when its date passes?
  • Is the reachability value readable by a screen reader and understandable without colour?
How it works
  1. Decide it needs a card. The destination matters to staff, and we can neither crawl it (PAT-001) nor copy it.
  2. Create it from the core content type. A content type built from the CMS core, packaged as a CMS Recipe. No extra module.
  3. Fill in the fields. Name and what it is for (both languages); who can use it; how to reach it; the owner position; the review date; shared taxonomy terms.
  4. Set reachability. One of a fixed value set: Reachable from this network, Not reachable from this network, or By another route (phone, form, in person). Each value has an official English and French name.
  5. Show the right thing. If reachable, the card has the link. If not, it says where and how to reach it, and never shows a link that will not open.
  6. Review it like any page. The owner is reminded by the review date. At review the owner chooses to keep, refresh or retire. Retiring follows the normal archive path.
  7. Measure it. Analytics records views and the outbound click per language. A card with many views and no clicks is telling us something.
Definition cards

Hover or focus any dotted term in this page to see the same definition.

Reference card

A short CMS page that stands for something staff need but we cannot crawl or link to. It says what it is, who it is for and how to reach it, and it has an owner and a review date.

Why it matters hereIt turns an invisible failure (a dead link) into owned, searchable, measurable content.

Owner of record

The named position accountable for a piece of content: for its accuracy, its review date and the decision to keep, refresh or retire it.

Why it matters hereA position, not a person, so a departure does not orphan the content.

Fixed value set

A short, closed list of allowed values for a label that comes from another system. Each value has an English and a French name set by us.

Why it matters hereStops raw text from another system appearing in one language only (Bilingual parity).

Content type

A template for a kind of page in the CMS, with its own fields (for example News item or Reference card).

Why it matters hereA content type built from the CMS core needs no extra module to patch.

CMS Recipe

A packaged, repeatable bundle of CMS configuration, such as a content type with its fields and permissions.

Why it matters hereA new content type is a reviewed change in version control, not a setting someone changed in production.

Shared taxonomy

One controlled list of terms (topics, divisions, content types) kept in both official languages and applied to content.

Why it matters hereThe same terms drive CMS pages, search facets and analytics reports. It is what lets us ask 'which HR pages are stale, and who owns them?'

Journey map

A picture of one person's path through a task, step by step, with what they do, think and feel at each step.

Why it matters hereIt shows where an experience breaks, so we fix the right step and can test it with real staff.

Wireframe

A plain sketch of a screen that shows what goes where. It leaves out final colours, wording and branding on purpose.

Why it matters herePeople can agree on layout, language switch and reading order before anything is built, and a vendor can show the same screen back.

Trade-offs

What we gain

  • A search for 'pay' finds something useful instead of nothing.
  • The gap has an owner and a review date, so it gets reviewed.
  • Same lifecycle as other content: no special process.
  • Cheap to build: a core content type and a recipe.

What it costs

  • Each card is one more page to review.
  • A card can drift from the real system if the owner does not review it.
  • It describes, it does not connect. Staff still leave the intranet to act.

When not to use it

  • The destination is reachable and we do not own it. Use a plain link, and use PAT-001 if it should be searchable.
  • We own the content. Write it as a normal page.
  • A single sentence in an existing page covers it. A card is for destinations people look for by name.
Checks every pattern must pass

Bilingual (official languages law)

Both language versions are required before the card publishes: the page gate already stops a card with no partner. Reachability is a fixed set of values, each translated by us, so no raw text from another system appears in one language. The name of the target system uses its official name in each language.

Accessible (WCAG 2.1 AA)

A card is plain design system page content. Reachability is written as text, never a coloured dot alone. A link, where there is one, says where it goes and names the system.

On-premises, no outside connection

A card is a page in our own CMS. It makes no outside call, and does not check whether an outside destination is up. A destination outside the isolated network is described, never linked.

How to test a vendor proposal

Ask the vendor to show, not tell. The pattern is met if the answer to each is yes.

  • Is a reference card built from the CMS core, with no extra module?
  • Can a card be published only with both an English and a French version?
  • Is reachability a fixed set of values, translated in both languages?
  • Does the page show no link when the destination cannot be reached?
  • Does a card have a mandatory owner position and review date, and show in the content health report?
  • Does a search in French find the French card, and in English the English card?

Proposed decision record

Unreachable destinations are published as reference cards

A destination staff need that cannot be crawled or linked from the isolated network is published as a reference card: an owned, reviewed, bilingual page that describes it. A bare link to an unreachable destination is not allowed.
