The first release of a bilingual intranet that runs entirely on the organization’s own isolated network. This page shows what is built, in what order, and how the work breaks down. It is a planning baseline, so it uses week numbers, not dates.

New here? Start with Baselines for an agile product. It explains how a plan like this one gives executives something firm to approve and still leaves the team room to learn.

What Phase 1 builds

Phase 1 is the first release: publish information, find it, and prove it is current and owned. Everything runs on departmental infrastructure with no outside connections.

Publish

A CMS with a government design system theme. One workflow carries English and French together. Every page has a named owner and a review date.

Find

A search engine with separate English and French indexes. One shared vocabulary runs through the site, search and analytics. A people directory comes from the identity feed.

Prove

A self-hosted analytics tool and content health reports. Video plays in the browser. Retired pages are archived to the records system after approval.

Existing content is migrated, not rebuilt. Nothing moves without an owner.

Roadmap

Four phases, twelve branches, nine gates. Dark bars are on the critical path: a delay there delays go-live. Light bars have float. Each gate is accepted against evidence, such as a working system, a test result or a signed authorization. Never against a progress report.

One rule for gate G6. Do not close testing until the bilingual parity report counts English and French separately. A combined pass rate can hide a stalled French queue. That is an official-languages exposure that only shows after go-live.

01

Today: an intranet nobody trusts

An employee needs the town hall video. Seven steps later, they may still not see it.

As is
7 steps
  1. Click a link on the intranet
  2. Land in the corporate document system
  3. Sign in
  4. Save the file
  5. Find the file
  6. Open it in a desktop player
  7. Hope the right codec is installed
→
At go-live
2 steps
  1. Click the page
  2. It plays in the browser, with captions in your language

One managed copy, with an owner and a review date. Views are measured.

The video is the visible symptom. Underneath it, pages have no named owner, nobody knows what is read, and nothing says when a page goes stale.

02

“One-stop shop” is four jobs

Each job costs a different amount. Phase 1 attacks one of them on purpose.

Find

I can locate information fast, in my language.

Directly attacked

Know

I learn what changed without hunting for it.

Partly Current and owned. Nothing is pushed yet.

Do

I start my task: pay, leave, travel, an IT ticket.

Barely Links only.

Connect

I can respond, ask, or contribute.

One channel “Was this useful?” on every page.

Why this order. Engagement features on stale content make the problem worse, because they advertise neglect. Fix the content first.

03

The road to go-live

Content moves before the doors open. When the new site goes live, the old one goes dark.

Discover

  • Workshops: content, IT, security
  • Shared taxonomy agreed
  • Content audit and owner list
  • Publishing tiers mocked up and agreed

Alpha on a connected network

  • Core concepts only, no real data
  • Teams trained on the real tools
  • Migration patterns tried
  • One and done

Isolated build

  • Development and staging on the isolated network
  • Content migrated to staging
  • Security hardening, authorization
  • Performance and accessibility checks

Go-live

  • Final content cutover
  • Old site switched off
  • Owners, review dates and reports running
  • Support in place
Content migration thread
DiscoverAudit. Every page gets an owner or a retire decision.
AlphaTry the migration on sample pages.
Isolated buildMigrate owned content to staging, English and French together.
Go-liveCut over. Nothing is carried for old bookmarks.

The rule: nothing migrates without an owner. The old site is not worth redirect work, so bookmarks are not preserved.

04

What go-live gives everyone

Six promises, all in scope for Phase 1.

Current and owned

  • Owner and review date on every page
  • Automatic keep, refresh or retire reminders
  • Approved retirement archived as a record

Findable

  • Separate English and French search indexes
  • One vocabulary across site, search and analytics
  • People directory from the identity feed
  • Empty searches logged and reviewed

Bilingual, provably

  • English and French measured separately, always
  • A one-language page shows as a gap the same day
  • No total can hide a stalled French queue

Automation

  • Anything on a schedule is the system’s job
  • Scheduled publish and unpublish
  • One workflow for both languages
  • Reports arrive by email

Transparency

  • Readership on the page, for its publisher
  • Executives see if a message was read, by language
  • The same numbers for everyone

Accessibility

  • Accessible by construction, from design-system components
  • Checked at build and again on the live site
  • Open findings counted per language

07

What we say first

Some things staff lose or do not get. Better they hear it from us.

Not in this release

  • Nothing is pushed: no digests or notifications to staff
  • No transactions: pay, leave and IT requests are links
  • No targeted announcements or follow-a-topic
  • No personalization, no mobile
  • Live streams link out to the existing platform

Ruled out, not deferred

  • Open discussion forums: moderation, language and records risk
  • Wiki-style co-editing: a page everyone edits is a page nobody owns
  • A separate engagement platform: another system to secure and run, outside the ownership model

Analytics follows the same path: year 1 asks whether the site is used and healthy, year 2 reads the same data better (is search working?), and year 3 asks whether people got the task done.

Concepts

Concepts are ideas that start a conversation. Only the first group, the mock-up set, is proposed Phase 1 scope. The rest are not committed. Each depends on skilled people being in place, such as a content manager, a taxonomy steward, a designer and a developer. Until those posts are filled, the ideas have no owner.

ProposedMock-ups for the publishing and lifecycle experience

Nobody agrees a publishing authority model in the abstract. People argue until they see a screen. These eight mock-ups settle who approves what, with Communications and two business lines, before the build packages start.

#ScreenThe question it answers
1“My content” owner dashboardWhat does accountability look like on a Tuesday morning?
2Communications review queueCan a small team carry review after publication? What does two days of work look like?
3Flag and escalationIs “comment, correct by date, unpublish” proportionate, or does it feel punitive?
4Publish screen, blockedIs the guardrail helpful or infuriating? Does it say what to do next?
5Tier indicatorHow does an author know, before writing, who approves this?
6Review-date decisionIs there enough evidence to decide, or will everyone press “keep”?
7Retire and archive confirmationDoes the irreversible step feel safe enough to take?
8“While you were away” digestDoes the site give something back to the people who maintain it?

What a mock-up must show to be useful: both languages side by side (French runs 15 to 30 percent longer); real content, including stale pages; the unhappy path, such as a page with no owner or a French version never written; realistic volume, because 6 pages look fine and 300 are a different product; and keyboard and screen-reader behaviour, even at concept stage.

Where they live: clickable HTML prototypes built on the design system and hosted inside the network. Cloud design tools would put content and structure outside the network. Prototypes must not become the build. They have no caching, no permissions and no tests. Reuse the markup and the pattern, not the code.

Ideas for later

Idea“What changed while I was away”

An employee returns from three weeks of leave. The home page says what changed in their areas, what videos were posted, and what they own that needs attention.

Welcome back. You were away 12 days.
4 updated pages in your areas (Leave, Telework) · 2 new videos, including the Q3 town hall · 1 page you own is due for review

Built from the CMS, not the analytics tool, which stays anonymized. The panel loads separately from the cached page.

IdeaThe site changes through the day

Mornings want news and notices. Midday is for tasks. Late in the day there is room for learning. Three time bands means three cached versions of the home page, not one per person. Test with analytics first: if traffic does not shift by time of day, the idea does not earn its keep.

IdeaSearch that shows whether a page can be trusted

Each result shows its owner and when it was last reviewed, so staff can tell current guidance from stale before clicking. A one-question “Did this answer your question?” turns search into a measure of task success.

Search: parental leave
Parental leave: how to apply · How-to · HR · Reviewed 2 months ago
Leave: planning your time off · Guidance · HR · Review due

Owner and review date are already Phase 1 fields. Showing them in results is a small step.

Proposed“My content”: owners see how their pages are doing

An owner opens one page and sees everything they publish: readers in each language, how people found each page, whether it was useful, and what is due for review. No request to IT, no waiting for a report. This one became load-bearing: notices carry no content, only a pointer, so this screen is the whole experience.

One page is overdue and almost unread, so it is a candidate to retire. Retiring is the owner’s decision. Nothing is removed automatically.

The data already exists in Phase 1: analytics visits and CMS metadata joined nightly. Figures are aggregate. No individual reader is shown.

IdeaSuccess-based dashboard for Communications

One view that answers “is the intranet healthy?”: ownership, freshness, parity and findability, with a drill-down by division. Phase 1 delivers the data through content health reporting. This idea is the presentation layer on top.

Also on the board: monthly owner nudges by email, and “retire with confidence”, which shows 12 months of traffic and the searches that led to a page before it is removed.

Why branch 12.0 exists. Publishing experience used to be spread across the design, build and content branches with no single owner. That is the gap where a good author experience and Communications’ control get traded off by accident. If that model changes afterwards, every ownership decision re-opens.

Scope

In scope for Phase 1

  • CMS built on its core, no distribution
  • Design-system theme, assets self-hosted
  • Bilingual publishing, parity measured per language
  • Search with separate English and French indexes
  • Self-hosted analytics, plus content freshness and ownership reporting
  • Video playback in the browser, with captions
  • People directory from the identity feed
  • Automated archiving of retired pages to the records system, after owner and publisher approval
  • Offline build pipeline, alpha on a connected network, isolated-network install, authorization, go-live

Out of scope, later release candidates

  • Service catalogue batch sync
  • “My tasks”, near real time
  • Personalization and role-based targeting
  • A BI tool as the content health surface

These are sized into the platform so it does not need rework later.
