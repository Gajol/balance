Pattern catalog / PAT-009

Automation can notice a problem and raise a flag. Only a named person decides to keep, fix or retire the thing.

  • Status: Proposed, pending EA confirmation
  • Version 0.1 · 2026-10-24
  • Applies: P3, P4, P5
  • Main layer: Content and governance

Supports: Automation raises flags, and a named person decides keep, refresh or retire.

  • Ownership and freshness
  • Messages and reminders

Found by the Solution Architect in the solution architecture (principle P3, content disposition in and, and the flag-and-escalate steps in WBS 12.5). Not named by the EA. Offered as a candidate pattern, pending EA confirmation.

The rule

DO

Let automation raise a flag with a plain reason, put it in front of the owner of record with a due date, and escalate by role if no one answers. A person chooses keep, refresh or retire.

DON'T

Let a job unpublish, delete or rewrite content because a rule said so. A wrong rule then removes something people rely on, and no one is accountable.

When this comes up

  • The intranet finds problems by itself: a page past its review date, a file that failed a scan, a term with no mapping, a file with no text.
  • The easy fix is to act: hide it, delete it, tag it. A wrong rule then acts at scale and unseen.
  • Content disposition needs a person. Automation helps people see; it does not decide (P3).

What pulls in different directions:

  • Speed and tidiness, against accountability.
  • Automation at scale, against the cost of one wrong removal.

Picture it

The experience

A content owner sees what needs their attention, why, and by when, and decides in three clicks. Nothing disappears behind their back.

Design rules for this pattern

  • Say why the flag exists, in plain words.
  • Offer exactly three choices.
  • Never change the page until a person chooses.
  • Say what happens if no one answers.

How to test the experience

Check these with real staff, in English and in French, and with a screen reader. The pattern is met if the answer to each is yes.

  • Does a flag leave the page published and unchanged until someone chooses?
  • Does every flag show a reason, an owner position and a due date?
  • Does an unanswered flag reach Communications on the due date?
  • Is the queue usable by keyboard and screen reader, with state in words?
  • Are open flags counted for English and French separately?

How it works

  1. List the flag types. Each has a plain-words reason: review overdue, file held, term unmapped, text missing. Keep the list short.
  2. Never change state. A flag does not unpublish, delete, move or rewrite. It adds a mark and a queue entry.
  3. Name who decides. Every flag goes to an owner of record, a position, with a due date.
  4. the notification service with a pointer. Use PAT-004: say that a flag was raised and link to it.
  5. Offer three choices. Keep (with a new review date), refresh, or retire. Retire follows the archive path (Archive retired content to the records system as PDF/A).
  6. Escalate by role. If the due date passes, the flag moves to Communications. A person there decides. Communications can unpublish as a person acting, not as a rule.
  7. Log the decision. Who, what, when, and the reason. Reports show flags open by age.
  8. Measure per language. Open flags, age and decisions are counted for English and French separately.

Definition cards

Hover or focus any dotted term in this page to see the same definition.

Owner of record

The named position accountable for a piece of content: for its accuracy, its review date and the decision to keep, refresh or retire it.

Why it matters hereA position, not a person, so a departure does not orphan the content.

Review date

The date by which the owner must confirm the content is still right, or refresh it, or retire it.

Why it matters hereFeeds the content health report. Past this date the content is flagged as stale and the owner is told.

Content health report

A scheduled report that joins CMS metadata (owner, review date, language) with analytics traffic to show what is stale, unowned or unread.

Why it matters hereIt is the evidence that content is owned and being refreshed or disposed of, per language.

Tombstone

A marker saying 'this item was removed at the source'. The index uses it to drop the item instead of keeping a result that leads nowhere.

Why it matters hereWithout tombstones, searches keep returning pages that no longer exist.

Service blueprint

A picture of a service in layers: what the person sees, and what staff and systems do out of sight to make it happen. A line of visibility separates the two.

Why it matters hereMost intranet failures happen behind the line, such as a stale index or a missed review. The blueprint puts an owner on each one.

Wireframe

A plain sketch of a screen that shows what goes where. It leaves out final colours, wording and branding on purpose.

Why it matters herePeople can agree on layout, language switch and reading order before anything is built, and a vendor can show the same screen back.

Trade-offs

What we gain

  • No silent removals.
  • Every decision has a name and a date.
  • Rules can be wrong without harm.
  • The same queue works for many kinds of flag.

What it costs

  • People must work the queue. Communications gains a daily duty.
  • Slower than automatic cleanup.
  • Flags can pile up if owners are not named.
  • Needs a due date and an escalation rule for every flag type.

When not to use it

  • A file fails a malware scan. Hold it at once (PAT-002), then flag it. Holding is not deleting.
  • An unpublish is needed now. Communications can do it as a person, with a record.

Checks every pattern must pass

Bilingual (official languages law)

A flag on one language version shows on the pair, and the choice covers both. Open flags and their age are counted per language. Reason text comes from a fixed set, so it is reviewed in both languages.

Accessible (WCAG 2.1 AA)

The queue is a real table with headers. State is written in words (Open, Overdue, Decided), not by colour alone. Each action is a button with a clear name.

On-premises, no outside connection

Flags, the queue and the log are CMS core features and configuration. Notifications use the internal mail relay. Nothing leaves the network.

How to test a vendor proposal

Ask the vendor to show, not tell. The pattern is met if the answer to each is yes.

  • Can you show that no flag type changes the state of the content?
  • Does every flag name an owner position and a due date?
  • Does an overdue flag escalate by role, and who acts on it?
  • Can you show the decision log with who, what and when?
  • Does retire follow the archive path in both languages?
  • Are open flags and their age reported per language?

Proposed decision record

Automation flags; people decide

No automated job unpublishes, deletes or rewrites content. A job may only raise a flag for a named owner of record, who chooses keep, refresh or retire. Only a person in Communications may unpublish out of turn, and the act is logged.
