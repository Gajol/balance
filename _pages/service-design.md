Government services are built for people who did not choose to use them. They have a deadline, a form, and not much patience. Good service design starts from that fact. These notes follow the thread from the Canadian Digital Service (CDS), which was set up to change how the federal government designs and delivers services, to the everyday practices it relies on: user research, interface design and accessibility.

They are working notes. Some are several years old, so check any link or policy before relying on it.

## Where it started

CDS was created after the Auditor General found that online services were not built around the needs of Canadians. Its job is to partner with departments, build working prototypes, and leave behind the skills to keep doing it.

- [GC Canadian Digital Service]({% post_url 2023-03-29-Canadian-Digital-Service %}) &mdash; the short overview. Read this first.
- [CDS]({{ '/application/cds/CDS-Canadian-Digital-Service' | relative_url }}) &mdash; the longer notes: the mandate, how CDS came about, how it organized for digital, an evaluation, and how other countries did the same thing.
- [CDS Hiring Guide]({{ '/application/cds/cds-digital-hiring-guides' | relative_url }}) &mdash; the best view of what a digital team is made of. It describes the roles CDS hires for: product managers, design researchers, designers and developers. Useful if you are building a team, or deciding what to ask a vendor for.

## Designing for people

Before anyone draws a screen, someone has to find out what the person on the other side needs. These notes cover how that is done.

- [User Experience]({{ '/application/cds/User-Experience-Overview' | relative_url }}) &mdash; a starting point on what user experience covers.
- [CDS User Experience]({{ '/application/cds/User-Experience-CDS' | relative_url }}) &mdash; how a digital service team does user research: interviewing, usability testing, and keeping research going through the life of a product. It also covers the designer toolkit.
- [User Interface Design]({{ '/application/cds/User-Interface' | relative_url }}) &mdash; the human-computer interaction ideas behind a good screen, and the design patterns that save you from reinventing one.
- [Design Thinking]({{ '/application/cds/Design-Thinking' | relative_url }}) &mdash; the problem-solving approach behind much of this work, and the people who shaped it.

## Designing the whole service

A good screen can still sit on top of a bad service. Service design looks at the full path, including the parts the client never sees.

- [Service Design UK Government]({{ '/application/cds/Service-Design-UK-Government' | relative_url }}) &mdash; pointers to the UK's government service design work, which CDS and many others learned from.

For a worked example of that idea, see [From strawman to blueprint]({{ '/journey-map-and-service-blueprint/' | relative_url }}). It follows one client through a service, then shows the staff work and systems behind each step.

## Making it work for everyone

A service that some people cannot use is not finished. Accessibility has to be part of the work from the first conversation, not a check at the end.

- [Accessibility]({{ '/application/cds/Accessibility' | relative_url }}) &mdash; a reference list: what the Government of Canada, companies and other countries publish, and the digital standards to build against.
- [CDS Accessibility]({{ '/application/cds/CDS-Accessibility' | relative_url }}) &mdash; accessibility stage by stage, from discovery (recognize bias and test with people) through alpha, where you validate with automated checks, to beta and live.
